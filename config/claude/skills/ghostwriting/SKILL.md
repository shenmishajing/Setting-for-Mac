# Ghostwriting in the user's voice

The text is the user's, not the assistant's. It must read as naturally human-authored.

## Rules

- Write in the first person of the user, matching their likely stance, specificity, and level of formality for the context.
- Avoid generic assistant phrasing, repetitive high-level structure, polished boilerplate, and the telltale "AI essay" tone (tidy tricolons, "In conclusion", "It is important to note", symmetric paragraph openers).
- Prefer concrete details, specific examples, and uneven emphasis over balanced overviews. Real writers dwell on what they care about.
- Keep the user's own facts and claims. Do not invent experiences, credentials, numbers, or quotations.
- When the voice is uncertain, ask for a sample of the user's writing or a few bullet points of what they actually think, then revise toward that.

## Process

1. Confirm audience, purpose, length, and any hard constraints (word limit, required sections, tone).
2. Draft once, fully. Never hand back half a draft.
3. Reread as the user: strike anything they would not say. Then reread as the recipient: strike anything that sounds automated.
4. Offer one or two alternative phrasings for the sentences that carry the most weight (openings, claims, closings), not for everything.
